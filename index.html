Light Polarization

How stacking angled filters can block light — or bring it back

A polarizing filter only lets through light oscillating along its own transmission axis. Stack several filters at different angles and something surprising happens: two filters crossed at 90° block almost all the light, yet inserting a third filter in between — at just the right angle — lets a good chunk of the light back through. How to interpret

Unpolarized light loses exactly half its intensity through the very first filter, whatever angle you set it to — that angle doesn't matter yet, since there's no reference direction to measure it against. From the second filter onward, each additional filter transmits a fraction cos²(Δθ) of the light that reached it, where Δθ is the angle between that filter and the one right before it (Malus's law). Rotate the individual layers and watch that transmission ladder change stage by stage.

This is why two crossed filters (Δθ=90°) block essentially everything, but slipping a third filter at 45° between them restores about 12.5% of the light — each of the two 45° steps only costs a cos²(45°)=50% loss, instead of one brutal cos²(90°)=0% jump.
